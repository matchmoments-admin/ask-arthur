## Post body

Someone knocks on your door with a laminated ID and a donation tin.

Two minutes ago, you'd never heard of the charity.

Here's how to check them in 60 seconds →

Australians are generous — and scammers know it. Fake charities spike after every flood, fire and cyclone, and around every big appeal. The pitch works because saying no to a good cause feels wrong.

But real charities in Australia are registered, and the register is public. We just made checking it fast:

— Type the name at askarthur.au/charity-check
— Arthur checks it against all 66,484 registered Australian charities (ACNC register, refreshed daily)
— It also catches the lookalikes: "Astralian Red Cross" — one letter off — comes back high-risk, not "no results found"

That last part is the point. A fake charity's name is designed to be one squint away from a real one. A plain register search just says "no results". Arthur says: this looks like a deliberate near-miss of a real charity — and here's the real one.

Doorknock, phone call, disaster appeal, street collector: the move is the same. Take the name. Check it. Then give with confidence.

Free, no signup.

If you've got a parent who gives to everything: send them this before the next appeal season.
