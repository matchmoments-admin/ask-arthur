# Ask Arthur — small-start monetisation and task backlog

Prepared 7 September 2026. Based on the local feature review, current source inspection, NORTH_STAR and the existing Arthur’s Watch plan. This is a proposed execution plan: no campaigns, messages, subscriptions, purchases, deployments or issue-tracker tasks have been launched.

## Recommendation

**One free weekly newsletter, one repeatable content format, one paying offer.** Start with Arthur’s Watch for Australians who want to recognise scams and help their families. Use LinkedIn for business reach and partner distribution for consumer reach. Add a limited YouTube experiment once the newsletter cadence works. Monetise first through the existing Brand Monitor pilot; a small organisation-funded workshop is a lower-technical-dependency alternative if the pilot acceptance gates take longer.

Keep everyday checking and urgent recovery guidance free. Early revenue should pay for protection, not restrict access to it. The existing platform is broad enough: demand, trust, predictable delivery and demonstrated outcomes are the near-term constraints.

Assumptions: founder-led; no confirmed audience/revenue baseline from this session; roughly **8–10 hours/week for growth and delivery**, plus separately allocated engineering time for launch blockers. The previous **10 reviewer-day** deep review is additional work, not squeezed into that weekly content budget. Use one paid experiment at a time if capacity is tighter. All proposed prices and targets below are experiments, not market benchmarks or revenue forecasts. Monetary examples are AUD; receipts are before tax, payment fees, hosting/provider costs and founder labour.

### Newsletter promise and format

Suggested promise: **“Five minutes each week to spot the scams affecting Australians—and one thing you can do to stay safer.”**

One issue, 500–700 words, manually reviewed:

1. A short human introduction from Brendan.
2. One current AU scam: how it works, the evidence and how to recognise it.
3. One original Ask Arthur observation where evidence supports it, explicitly scoped to the platform’s sample.
4. One practical action a reader can take today.
5. A main CTA to check suspicious content; a small forward/share prompt and a reply invitation.

Maintain a public archive on the existing site. Use independently verified regulator sources when original observations are thin. Never turn a small dataset into a claim about nationwide prevalence. Competitor newsletter intake stays private intelligence under the existing plan; write original explanations and verify claims against primary evidence. Human approval remains before publication. A quiet week can be an evergreen lesson rather than fabricated urgency.

First four issue/video pairs (working topics, not claims of current incidents):

| Week | Newsletter lesson                                   | 3–5 minute video                                                       | Main action                                  |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | How to check a suspicious message                   | Walk through a synthetic parcel-message example                        | Try the free checker                         |
| 2    | How a convincing clone shop misleads buyers         | Compare redacted example signals without visiting live malicious pages | Check before paying                          |
| 3    | Marketplace deposits and verification-code requests | Explain two tactics using clearly labelled examples                    | Pause and verify independently               |
| 4    | What to do after a suspicious interaction           | Demonstrate the existing recovery/reporting journey                    | Use the relevant existing next-step guidance |

Record a real explanation or screen demonstration with narration. Do not invest in a studio, daily posting or automated content volume. Use screenshots/synthetic examples; remove identifying information and do not publish live clickable malicious URLs.

## Small paid offers

### A. Brand Monitor founding pilot

Use the existing A$300/month, first-month-free offer. Cap the experiment at three organisations. A proposed narrow scope to validate against actual coverage:

- One AU brand, its agreed legitimate domains and an agreed impersonation watchlist.
- Automated monitoring within explicitly stated source coverage and cadence.
- Human-reviewed relevant alerts during stated business hours.
- Monthly evidence/outcome report.
- Escalation through supported channels where authorised; third-party response/removal is not guaranteed.

Specify exclusions: no 24/7 incident response, no guaranteed removal, no recovery of stolen funds, no unrestricted investigation requests. Before onboarding, calculate whether provider costs and founder time fit A$300. Secure explicit brand authority and verified org ownership. The free first month needs a scheduled review and an agreed paid-conversion decision; it is not an indefinite free service.

First prospect segment hypothesis: **small-to-medium AU ecommerce brands with a verified, relevant impersonation concern and an identifiable decision-maker**. Prefer a narrow segment over banks/government initially. Use current evidence, not old alerts or speculative accusations. Start with a manually qualified list of 20 organisations; aim for five discovery conversations and one scoped pilot. These are activity targets, not expected conversion rates.

### B. Organisation-funded workshop alternative

Offer a 45-minute “Spot the scam before you click or pay” session plus a one-page checklist. Test A$250–500 with an employer, association, community organisation with a budget, or a sponsor funding a community session. One free rehearsal is enough to test the teaching format. Booking and payment can be manual using established business tools; no course platform needed.

The paying customer is the host, not a person seeking urgent help. Keep the scope educational. Attendee newsletter signup is separately voluntary; do not import an attendee list. Reuse the session and measure preparation time, attendee usefulness and voluntary signups. If no host pays after ten qualified conversations, change the offer or stop the experiment.

### C. Sponsorship later

Create a one-page media sheet only once there are real delivered-email counts and engagement. Offer one clearly labelled placement, fixed duration and a report of actual results. A suggested A$150–300/four-issue founding test is willingness-to-pay discovery; it is not justified by subscriber count alone. If there is little audience, sell sponsorship of a concrete hosted session instead of selling unproven impressions.

Publish a short editorial independence rule: sponsorship cannot buy a verdict, suppress a finding or receive subscriber data. Do not sell “verified safe” badges or priority treatment in scam scoring. Avoid sponsors whose revenue depends on frightening victims into recovery or protection purchases.

## Decision gates and weekly capacity

**Weeks 1–4:** delivery and relevance. Aim for 50–100 voluntary subscribers, four issues, ten discovery conversations total and either a scoped pilot or one workshop booking. These are small internal goals. If below them, distinguish insufficient outreach from a weak offer; do not declare market failure from an unpromoted list.

**Weeks 5–8:** useful engagement and willingness to pay. Look for actual replies, repeat checking, partner shares and at least one paying customer or host. After 20 qualified buyer conversations without willingness to pay, revise the audience/problem/offer before further product development. After four videos, retain YouTube only if qualified actions or reusable teaching value justify its time.

**Weeks 9–12:** repeatable delivery. Aim for one to three paid customers with affordable service time or a repeat workshop booking. Test sponsorship only with real audience evidence. If no one renews or delivery is uneconomic, narrow scope/change price; do not automate an offer buyers do not value.

Use clicks, replies, successful checks and renewals as primary signals. Email opens alone are not a sufficient success measure. Define repeat usage using privacy-preserving existing identifiers; do not claim individuals across devices can be perfectly deduplicated. Revenue reporting distinguishes invoiced, collected, recurring, trial and one-off amounts.

Suggested weekly growth allocation after launch gates:

- 2.5 hours: research, write and approve the newsletter.
- 1 hour: LinkedIn adaptations and partner coordination.
- 2 hours: prospect/reader conversations and follow-up.
- 1.5 hours: pilot delivery or workshop preparation.
- 1 hour: scorecard, support and operational checks.
- Up to 2 hours: optional video experiment; omit it if delivery exceeds the cap.

No new paid marketing software initially; proposed incremental growth-tool budget ceiling A$100/month, excluding existing infrastructure and necessary remediation. This is a cap, not a spending target. Use current equipment. Measure existing infrastructure spend separately so a low marketing budget does not disguise an expensive product.

## Do not build yet

Mobile revival, Phone Footprint expansion, Breach Defence suite, another generic AI scanner, a new newsletter CMS, an affiliate/referral engine, a course platform, a membership community, daily automated videos, a complex sales CRM or a new analytics warehouse. Capture manual evidence first. Do not reintroduce on-page programmatic ads, which the current strategy explicitly excludes.
